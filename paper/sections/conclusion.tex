\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We tracked simulator-trained quantum classifiers on two IBM Heron processors for
\val{campaign-days} days with a pre-registered protocol that fits in a free account. Calibration
emulators overstated hardware accuracy, session-to-session variation exceeded shot noise on at
least one device, and the cheap repairs closed only a small part of the gap, whereas calibrating
conformal prediction on hardware, or updating it online, restored coverage.
% claim:emu-optimistic claim:small-recovery claim:hwcal-over claim:aci-near
For small QML experiments this suggests repeating hardware runs on more than one day, preferring
repairs that need little or no QPU time, and calibrating uncertainty on hardware rather than in
simulation. Natural next steps are more devices and longer windows, larger models, and runs with
twirling and dynamical decoupling switched on.
